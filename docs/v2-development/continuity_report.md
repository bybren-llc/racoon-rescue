### ISSUE #1: VEHICLE TYPE DISCREPANCY

**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Type:** Physical Continuity / Prop Error
**Location:** Lines 226-227

**Description:**
The screenplay describes Danny's vehicle as:

```
His TRUCK waits at the far end. 2009 Dodge Charger. Silver. Paint oxidizing.
```

**Problem:** A Dodge Charger is a **sedan/muscle car**, not a truck. The script consistently refers to the vehicle as "truck" throughout (19+ occurrences), yet identifies it as a Charger.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Line 199: "I got a truck."
- Line 227: "2009 Dodge Charger"
- Line 248: "INT. DANNY'S TRUCK"
- Line 453: "EXT. DANNY'S TRUCK"
- Character Interview Q65: "2009 Dodge Charger"

**Recommendation:**
Change line 227 to a truck model:
```
His TRUCK waits at the far end. 2009 Dodge Ram. Silver. Paint oxidizing.
```

Alternative options: Ford F-150, Chevy Silverado, or Toyota Tacoma. The "truck" terminology has thematic resonance with Danny's working-class Bakersfield background and his father's truck-driving legacy.

**Priority:** CRITICAL - This will cause immediate confusion during production/prop acquisition.

### ISSUE #2: PHONE BATTERY STATE CONTRADICTION

**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Type:** Prop Continuity
**Location:** Lines 119-137 vs. Lines 1269-1286

**Description:**
At the bar, Danny's phone is explicitly established as DEAD:

```
Line 119-120: Danny's ancient FLIP PHONE sits on the bar, screen dark.
He picks it up. Tries to turn it on. Nothing.

Line 128-131: DANNY: You got a charger? The old kind?
LEON: Ain't had one of those since Bush.
```

However, at the crash site aftermath:

```
Lines 1271-1276: His PHONE lies on the asphalt beside him. Cracked screen.
Spider-web fractures across the display. But visible through the damage,
glowing faintly: RECENT CALLS / SPONSOR MIKE - DO NOT ANSWER AFTER 2100
```

**Problem:** The phone cannot display "RECENT CALLS" if it was dead and never charged. No charging opportunity is established between the bar and the crash.

**Note:** Lines 135-137 show the contact list was visible "before it died" - this is a flashback/memory insert, not the phone's current state.

**Recommendation Options:**

**Option A (Preferred):** Establish car charger use. Add after line 633:
```
Danny plugs his phone into a car charger. The screen flickers to life.
```

**Option B:** The phone remains dead. Change lines 1271-1280 to:
```
His PHONE lies on the asphalt beside him. Cracked screen. Dark. Dead.
He holds it anyway - the call he never made.
```

**Option C:** Impact jolts battery. Add at line 1271:
```
His PHONE lies on the asphalt beside him. The impact must have jolted
something - the screen flickers to life briefly, cracked but glowing.
```

**Priority:** CRITICAL - Affects a key thematic moment (Danny's inability to call for help).

### ISSUE #3: EAR INJURY TEMPORAL ERROR

**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Type:** Physical Continuity / Cause-Effect Logic
**Location:** Lines 976-978

**Description:**
During the attack sequence, the script states:

```
Lines 976-978: The raccoon's teeth find Danny's bandaged ear. The existing wound.
```

**Problem:** Danny should NOT have a bandaged ear or "existing wound" during the flashback attack sequence. The bandage is only present in the COURTROOM scenes (present day):

```
Line 28: A BANDAGE covers his left ear.
```

**Timeline Logic:**
1. Flashback begins: Danny at bar - NO ear injury
2. Raccoon attacks during drive - ear injury OCCURS HERE
3. Courtroom (14 hours later) - ear is NOW bandaged

The attack sequence CREATES the injury; it cannot reopen an "existing wound" that hasn't happened yet within the story's chronology.

**Recommendation:**
Change lines 976-978 to:
```
The raccoon's teeth find Danny's ear. CLAMP DOWN.
```

Remove "bandaged" and "existing wound" entirely - these are anachronistic references to the post-injury state.

**Priority:** CRITICAL - This breaks cause-and-effect logic and will confuse the injury's origin.

Took me ten years to find Margaret.

I just wanted one quiet year.

GERALD (to himself, almost wondering)
I just retired. I just wanted one quiet year.

**Priority:** MODERATE - Creates character confusion but doesn't break plot mechanics.

### ISSUE #7: DRESS WHITES WARDROBE LOGIC

**Severity:** MODERATE (NEW FINDING)
**Type:** Wardrobe Continuity
**Location:** Lines 1088-1089, 1124-1128

**Description:**

```
Lines 1088-1089: Blood. Scratches. Dress whites visible beneath his torn jacket.
Lines 1124-1128: Gerald's eyes drop to Danny's chest... The DRESS WHITE UNIFORM.
Navy insignia.
```

**Problem:** Danny is at a chain restaurant birthday party on a Sunday night (per character interview Q41: "Called mom at 1400 - Sunday routine"). He would NOT be wearing dress whites to drink at a Bennigan's-type establishment.

The work jacket over dress whites makes no practical sense - dress whites are formal/ceremonial attire, not worn casually and certainly not under a welding jacket.

**Recommendation:**
Either:

**Option A:** Danny is wearing civilian clothes (jeans, work shirt - consistent with Q68: "Jeans, work boots, whatever t-shirt's clean"). Remove dress white references from crash scene. The courtroom scene properly shows him in dress whites.

**Option B:** Establish a reason Danny is in dress whites (returning from formal event, ceremony, etc.). This would require adding context at the bar scene.

**Priority:** MODERATE - Affects visual logic but can be addressed in production if not scripted.
