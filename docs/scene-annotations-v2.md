### SCENE 1: COLD OPEN - COURTROOM

**Slug:** INT. NAVY JAG COURTROOM - DAY
**Estimated Pages:** 1.5
**Time in Story:** Present day (14 hours after main events)

#### Synopsis

A wounded Danny Vega sits in a military courtroom, unable to explain how a raccoon paw print became logged as evidence. The scene establishes mystery, consequences, and the frame narrative before flashing back to reveal how everything went wrong.

#### Key Beats

- Court Reporter frozen in disbelief establishes surreal tone
- Commander Wells displays "Prosecution's Exhibit Fourteen" - the paw print
- Danny's visible wounds (bandaged ear, scratches, thousand-yard stare)
- Gerald and Margaret in gallery - family tension implicit
- Danny's whispered confession: "It started with a letter"
- TITLE CARD: "RACOON RESCUE" with offscreen raccoon chitter

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Commander Wells (institutional authority)
- **Shift:** Power is static; Danny is completely subject to the court
- **End State:** Danny surrendered, system dominant

#### Thematic Function

Establishes that consequences are inevitable. The frame shows the "after" - Danny has already failed to fix this. The theme is encoded in his inability to explain, his acceptance of the outcome. The paw print as evidence literalizes the absurdity of trying to control chaos.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** AFTERMATH / ACCEPTANCE (End of journey, shown first)

Danny is post-transformation here. The stillness, the acceptance, the wounds - all evidence of a man who has stopped fighting. This is the destination shown before the journey.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:** Fluorescent institutional lighting (repeated in bar, contrasted with sodium vapor)
- **Refrain:** Danny's silence where "It's fine" would normally appear
- **Raccoon Presence:** Paw print as artifact; offscreen chitter in darkness
- **Foreshadowing:** Gerald's jaw working silently (pays off in final courtroom scene)

### SCENE 2: THE BAR

**Slug:** INT. BENNIGAN'S-TYPE CHAIN RESTAURANT - NIGHT
**Estimated Pages:** 3.0
**Time in Story:** 14 hours before courtroom (1:30 AM approx.)

#### Synopsis

Danny drinks alone at closing time in a depressing chain restaurant. His card is declined, his phone is dead, and all the safety nets in his life (sponsor Mike, AA chip, IID breathalyzer) are visible but unused. Leon the bartender extends compassion Danny cannot accept.

#### Key Beats

- Eight whiskey glasses lined up "like little soldiers"
- Dead flip phone reveals "SPONSOR MIKE - DO NOT ANSWER AFTER 2100" - he never called
- Card declined, revealing financial collapse
- Rachel's letter in pocket - won't discuss it
- Leon extends tab grace; Danny cannot receive kindness
- IID handset and six-month AA chip visible on keyring
- Exit past deflated party decorations - "40 AND FABULOUS"

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Danny (customer position, apparent control)
- **Shift:** Card decline strips illusion of control
- **End State:** Leon holds quiet moral authority; Danny retreats to his truck

#### Thematic Function

Danny's world is filled with systems designed to help him (sponsor, AA program, IID, bartender's kindness). He cannot use any of them. The theme "you can't fix everything" is inverted here - he won't let anything fix him. The sagging party decorations externalize his emotional state.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** ROCK BOTTOM PREPARATION (Pre-catalyst denial)

Danny is in deep denial, using the refrain "It's fine" to reject all help. His welder's tic (thumb/forefinger rubbing) shows unconscious anxiety. He has all the tools but cannot use them.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:** Fluorescent lights making everyone "look like they died three days ago"
- **Refrain:** "One more" / "Don't want food" - minimizing
- **Object Symbolism:**
  - Eight glasses = accumulated damage
  - Dead phone = severed connections
  - AA chip = abandoned program
  - Copenhagen can = oral fixation/substitution
- **Leon's Quiet Wisdom:** "Yeah. You got a truck." (Final line - devastating)

### SCENE 3: PARKING LOT APPROACH

**Slug:** EXT. BENNIGAN'S-TYPE CHAIN RESTAURANT - PARKING LOT - NIGHT
**Estimated Pages:** 1.0
**Time in Story:** 1:47 AM

#### Synopsis

Danny walks to his truck through an empty parking lot bathed in harsh sodium vapor light. Something rustles in a dumpster. He ignores it - the first time - and reaches his truck.

#### Key Beats

- Sodium vapor lighting transforms the mundane into the ominous
- Dumpster rustling - first hint of raccoon
- Passes minivan with "HONOR STUDENT" sticker (normal life he doesn't have)
- IID device catches light as he unlocks truck

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Danny (moving through space with purpose)
- **Shift:** Environment begins asserting itself (rustling, watching eyes)
- **End State:** Equilibrium - Danny has agency but something watches

#### Thematic Function

The parking lot is a liminal space between the bar (denial) and the truck (consequence). Danny's choice to ignore the rustling shows his pattern: avoid, proceed, don't investigate the darkness.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** THRESHOLD (Approaching point of no return)

Danny is moving toward the decision that will destroy/save him. His practiced walk past trouble shows habitual avoidance.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:** Sodium vapor orange (will recur throughout - harsh truth lighting)
- **Sound Design:** Rustling, then silence
- **Dumpster as Altar:** First establishment of the "garbage cathedral" visual
- **Contrast:** "HONOR STUDENT" sticker vs. Danny's trajectory

### SCENE 4: IID FAILURE

**Slug:** INT. DANNY'S TRUCK - CONTINUOUS
**Estimated Pages:** 0.5
**Time in Story:** Immediately following

#### Synopsis

Danny settles into his truck and blows into the IID breathalyzer. It fails. BAC .14. He is locked out of his own vehicle. Through the windshield, two reflective eyes watch from the dumpster.

#### Key Beats

- Truck interior details: Copenhagen smell, spit cups, unused car seat with tags
- IID FAIL - BAC .14 - LOCKOUT ACTIVE
- "Okay. This is fine. This is fine." (refrain, escalating)
- Raccoon eyes visible through windshield
- Danny exits toward the light

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Technology (IID has locked him out)
- **Shift:** Danny spots the watching eyes
- **End State:** Curiosity/desperation pulls him toward the dumpster

#### Thematic Function

The IID represents society's attempt to control Danny's behavior. Its success (it worked exactly as designed) is his failure. The car seat with tags is devastating - a future that never happened. The raccoon's eyes offer an escape from systems that are working correctly.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** CATALYST TRIGGER (The worst idea begins)

The IID failure is the inciting incident. Danny's response will define everything that follows.

#### Notable Elements

- **Refrain:** "This is fine" - three times, increasingly hollow
- **Visual Motif:** Red light of failure; distant orange glow of dumpster
- **Object Symbolism:**
  - Car seat with tags = lost future/family
  - Spit cups = ongoing addiction
  - IID camera = surveillance/accountability
- **Raccoon Eyes:** First direct visual contact (predator-prey inversion)

### SCENE 5: THE DUMPSTER / RACCOON ACQUISITION

**Slug:** EXT. DUMPSTER - CONTINUOUS / MOMENTS LATER
**Estimated Pages:** 2.0
**Time in Story:** ~2:00 AM

#### Synopsis

Danny approaches the dumpster, discovers an unconscious, food-drunk raccoon, and has his terrible eureka moment. He retrieves the animal with military precision, treating garbage diving as a tactical operation.

#### Key Beats

- Dumpster as "garbage cathedral" - religious lighting
- Discovery: fat, unconscious raccoon, belly-up among fast food debris
- Danny's eureka: "You breathe." (The plan crystallizes)
- Tactical recon: pebble toss, perimeter check, jacket preparation
- Dumpster dive executed with absurd professionalism
- Emergence with raccoon cradled "like precious cargo"
- Something drips from jacket - he doesn't look down

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Raccoon (unconscious but holding the key)
- **Shift:** Danny gains physical control of the animal
- **End State:** Danny believes he has power; reality will correct this

#### Thematic Function

This is Danny's attempt to "fix" an unfixable situation through absurd means. The military precision applied to raccoon theft satirizes his coping mechanism (hyper-competence masking collapse). The "garbage cathedral" imagery suggests false worship - he's kneeling before the wrong altar.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** COMMITMENT TO WRONG PATH (Doubling down on control)

Danny cannot accept the IID's verdict, so he manufactures a workaround. This is his pattern: don't surrender, don't ask for help, find another way to force the outcome you want.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:** Sodium vapor as religious/revelatory light
- **Refrain:** "Okay. Okay." - self-soothing under pressure
- **Physical Comedy:** Work jacket buttoned to collar, knuckles cracked - absurd preparation
- **Tonal Balance:** Horror-comedy - "Oh god what is that smell"
- **The Eureka Shot:** Danny's face shifting as the plan forms
- **Something Dripping:** He doesn't look = willful blindness to reality

### SCENE 6: THE IID BYPASS

**Slug:** INT. DANNY'S TRUCK - CONTINUOUS
**Estimated Pages:** 2.5
**Time in Story:** ~2:10 AM

#### Synopsis

Danny attempts to use the unconscious raccoon to bypass the breathalyzer. After several failed attempts, he discovers that squeezing the animal like a bellows produces results. The IID turns green. He buckles the raccoon into the passenger seat.

#### Key Beats

- Raccoon positioned at IID mouthpiece - nothing happens
- "Come on, buddy. Breathe for me." (coaxing tone)
- Manual mouth opening - "A surprising amount of tiny teeth"
- Chest squeeze solution - CPR for the morally compromised
- GREEN LIGHT / ENGINE AUTHORIZED
- "Holy shit it worked." (wrong kind of triumph)
- Seatbelt clicked over raccoon - the HERO SHOT established
- Departure: raccoon rides shotgun into the night

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** IID system (still blocking)
- **Shift:** Danny's ingenuity defeats the system
- **End State:** Danny has illusory power; raccoon remains an unknown variable

#### Thematic Function

Danny has "fixed" the problem through fraud and absurdity. The theme is present in negative - he CAN'T accept that he shouldn't drive, so he manufactures a solution. The seatbelting of the raccoon is the visual thesis: arranging chaos into the appearance of order.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** FALSE VICTORY (Hubris peak)

This is Danny's greatest triumph and worst mistake simultaneously. His relief is "pure, unearned, impossible" - the text names it directly.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:** Red to green light transition (false redemption)
- **Refrain:** "Holy shit it worked" - wonder + horror
- **Hero Shot:** Danny and buckled raccoon - absurdist buddy comedy setup
- **Teeth Detail:** "A surprising amount of tiny teeth" - foreshadowing
- **Physical Comedy:** Belly-squeeze technique, door struggle
- **Sound Design:** IID beeps, processing, the green light chime

### SCENE 7: THE CONFESSIONAL DRIVE

**Slug:** INT. DANNY'S TRUCK (MOVING) - NIGHT
**Estimated Pages:** 3.5
**Time in Story:** ~2:15-2:35 AM

#### Synopsis

Driving home with his unconscious accomplice, Danny begins talking to the raccoon. What starts as celebration becomes confession - his marriage, his panic attacks, his violence, his inability to ask for help. This is the emotional core of the film.

#### Key Beats

- Streetlights strobing: light/dark/light/dark (visual rhythm)
- "Rachel would've loved this." (confession begins unintentionally)
- Rachel left four months ago; Danny didn't fight it
- The letter: "scared of me" - he can't finish reading it
- Punching the door frame - broke two knuckles
- Panic attacks since 2012; Rachel was the only one who knew
- "I haven't had anyone to count with in four months"
- "I miss being small. I miss being twelve years old and not knowing how to fix anything and that being okay."
- Admission: "This is who I am... the guy who punches doors"
- "I need help." - first time spoken aloud
- "This is fine." - hollow now, no one believes it
- Raccoon's eyelids flicker - the calm before

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Danny (believes he's in control, confessing safely)
- **Shift:** Emotional vulnerability strips defenses
- **End State:** Danny exposed; raccoon beginning to stir

#### Thematic Function

**This scene is the thematic heart of the screenplay.**

Danny's confessions articulate the core wound: "I miss being twelve years old and not knowing how to fix anything and that being okay." The theme is stated explicitly here - the loss of permission to be unable to fix things. His confession to an unconscious animal is safe precisely because it can't respond, can't help, can't fix him. He finally says "I need help" - but to the one listener who cannot provide it.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** CONFRONTATION WITH TRUTH (Pre-surrender vulnerability)

Danny breaks through denial but cannot yet act. The confession is real; the context (unconscious raccoon) prevents it from connecting to help. This is the purgatorial moment between breakdown and breakthrough.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:** Streetlight strobe - confession in flashes, truth emerging incrementally
- **Refrain:** "This is fine" - finally hollow, even he doesn't believe it
- **Key Dialogue:**
  - "I miss being small"
  - "I need help"
  - "That's who I am"
- **Rachel Backstory:**
  - Left four months ago
  - Wrote a letter he can't finish
  - Scared of what he was becoming (not scared he'd hurt her)
  - Counted backwards from 100 during panic attacks
- **Welder's Tic:** Thumb/forefinger rubbing - checking for burns
- **AA Backstory:** "Second time in six months" driving drunk
- **Dramatic Irony:** Raccoon waking while Danny's eyes are on road

### SCENE 8: THE ATTACK

**Slug:** INT. DANNY'S TRUCK (MOVING) / CAREENING - NIGHT
**Estimated Pages:** 3.0
**Time in Story:** ~2:35-2:40 AM

#### Synopsis

The raccoon wakes up. What follows is vehicular chaos as a terrified wild animal attacks Danny at highway speed, culminating in the truck crashing through a fence into Gerald's backyard birthday party.

#### Key Beats

- Raccoon eyes OPEN - targeting system acquiring
- Danny makes eye contact - "Oh--"
- Raccoon LAUNCHES - claws rake face, blood on windshield
- Truck swerves across highway
- Raccoon's SCREAM: "part woman, part power tool, pure rage"
- Bite locks onto forearm; raccoon won't let go
- Emergency braking sends raccoon into windshield (cracks glass)
- Danny's ear (previous wound) targeted and reopened
- Gerald's fence explodes - paper lanterns scatter
- Beer pong table destroyed; truck stops at pool edge
- Raccoon exits through rear window
- Danny sits in thousand-yard stare as phones record

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Raccoon (total, immediate, violent)
- **Shift:** Neither party has control - pure chaos
- **End State:** Chaos resolves into stillness; Danny surrendered

#### Thematic Function

The raccoon is nature's rejection of Danny's control. You cannot kidnap chaos, buckle it in safely, and expect compliance. The attack is violent redemption - Danny is forced to stop controlling. The crash brings him to Gerald (father figure) and witnesses (accountability).

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** FORCED SURRENDER (Control stripped violently)

Danny literally cannot fix this. His hands leave the wheel. He stops fighting.

#### Notable Elements

- **Raccoon as Force of Nature:**
  - "A machine that will not stop"
  - Scream like nothing that should exist
  - Bent ear but still coming
- **Body Horror:** Blood, bite wounds, reopened ear
- **Visual Motif:** Red and blue anticipation (brake lights, then police lights)
- **Party Crash Details:**
  - Margaret's 50th birthday
  - "HAPPY 50TH MARGARET!" banner
  - Beer pong table destruction
  - Red solo cups "like spent shell casings"
  - Phone screens "like fireflies"
- **Gerald Introduction:** Hawaiian shirt, bag of ice dropping
- **Raccoon Exit:** Disappears into trees, chitters "almost like laughter"

### SCENE 9: THE AFTERMATH

**Slug:** EXT. GERALD'S BACKYARD - CRASH SITE - NIGHT / MOMENTS LATER
**Estimated Pages:** 2.5
**Time in Story:** ~2:45-3:00 AM

#### Synopsis

Danny sits on the curb surrounded by emergency response chaos. For the first time, he stops trying to fix anything. He holds his cracked phone, sees Mike's contact, and doesn't put it away.

#### Key Beats

- Blue and red lights washing over destruction
- Danny on curb: hands still, no welder's tic
- "For the first time... he is not trying to fix anything"
- Cop asks if he's injured; Danny admits: "Yeah. Yeah, I think so."
- Cracked phone shows "SPONSOR MIKE" contact
- He holds the phone. Doesn't dial. Doesn't put it away.
- No "It's fine." He doesn't say anything at all.
- "A man who has finally stopped running from the thing that was always going to catch him"
- Match cut: eyes close (parking lot) / eyes open (courtroom)

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Systems (police, medical, legal - all responding)
- **Shift:** Danny surrenders to systems instead of fighting
- **End State:** Acceptance - power ceded willingly

#### Thematic Function

**This is the thematic resolution.**

Danny stops. The hands are still. The refrain is absent. For the first time, he allows himself to be broken, to be helped, to not fix anything. The phone held but not dialed represents the threshold - he's facing the possibility of asking for help but hasn't yet. The theme completes: "You can't fix everything - and that's okay" becomes embodied in stillness.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** SURRENDER / TRANSFORMATION (The turn)

Danny has stopped running. The admission "Yeah. Yeah, I think so" (to being injured) is revolutionary for this character. He accepts being small, being broken, being unable to fix this.

#### Notable Elements

- **Visual Motif:**
  - Red/blue lights washing like waves (baptismal)
  - Danny small in frame, surrounded by machinery
- **Refrain Absence:** "It's fine" starts but cannot be finished
- **Physical Stillness:** Hands at rest, no tic
- **The Phone:** Cracked but functional - like Danny
- **Gerald/Margaret:** Present but distant - consequences witnessed
- **Match Cut:** Eyes close/open connects crash to courtroom

### SCENE 11: SENTENCING

**Slug:** INT. NAVY JAG COURTROOM - LATER / MOMENTS LATER
**Estimated Pages:** 2.0
**Time in Story:** Present day (sentencing)

#### Synopsis

Commander Wells reads the charges. Danny is asked for final words. He says only: "No, ma'am." Gerald weeps. Danny is led away, pausing to tell his father: "It's okay, Dad. I'm okay." For the first time, we believe him.

#### Key Beats

- Charges read: fraud, DUI, conduct unbecoming
- "Do you have anything to say before sentencing?"
- "No, ma'am." - acceptance, not defeat
- Gerald's tears; Margaret's hand squeezing his
- Audio fades during sentence (we see Gerald's reaction, know it's severe)
- Final exchange: "Danny--" / "It's okay, Dad. I'm okay."
- Danny led through door; Gerald alone in empty courtroom

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** Commander Wells / Court
- **Shift:** Danny's acceptance transforms the dynamic
- **End State:** Danny has internal power through surrender

#### Thematic Function

Danny's "No, ma'am" is the answer to "You can't fix everything." He won't try to explain, justify, or minimize. He accepts. His final words to Gerald - "I'm okay" - reclaim the refrain. It's no longer denial; it's truth earned through breakdown.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** TRANSFORMATION COMPLETE (New Danny emerges)

The man who could never say "I need help" now accepts consequences without resistance. The "I'm okay" is genuine because it's no longer trying to prevent help.

#### Notable Elements

- **Refrain Transformation:** "I'm okay" replaces "It's fine" - earned version
- **Gerald Payoff:** Father finally sees son accept help/consequence
- **Silence as Power:** "No, ma'am" is stronger than any speech
- **Visual:** Danny small, led away; Gerald alone
- **Emotional Core:** The "Danny--" / "It's okay, Dad" exchange

### SCENE 12: EPILOGUE

**Slug:** NEWSPAPER / EXT. ALLEY - DUMPSTER - NIGHT
**Estimated Pages:** 0.5
**Time in Story:** Unspecified future

#### Synopsis

Newspaper headline reveals the policy changes resulting from "the Vega incident." Title cards inform us Danny was dishonorably discharged, became an addiction counselor, and has been sober three years. Final image: raccoon eyes in the darkness, then gone.

#### Key Beats

- Headline: "NAVY INVESTIGATES 'WILDLIFE OVERRIDE' LOOPHOLE"
- Raccoon paw print stylized in graphic
- Title cards: discharge, new career, three years sober
- Fade to black, raccoon chitter
- Final image: dumpster, sodium vapor light, watching eyes
- Eyes disappear into shadow

#### Power Arc

- **Initial Power:** N/A (denouement)
- **Shift:** Story's impact ripples outward (policy change)
- **End State:** Bandit remains in the wild; Danny finds purpose

#### Thematic Function

The worst night of Danny's life created positive systemic change (the loophole closed) and personal transformation (addiction counselor, sober). The raccoon's final appearance suggests some things remain wild, uncontrollable - and that's okay. The chaos that destroyed him also freed him.

#### Character Arc Moment

**Stage:** NEW EQUILIBRIUM (Life after transformation)

Danny found purpose in helping others with addiction - the help he couldn't ask for himself. Three years sober indicates the transformation stuck.

#### Notable Elements

- **Legacy:** Policy change from personal disaster
- **Career Irony:** Man who couldn't call sponsor becomes counselor
- **Raccoon Closure:** Watching, then disappearing - chaos unresolved but accepted
- **Visual Bookend:** Dumpster/sodium light returns us to the beginning
- **Sound Design:** Raccoon chitter - last word belongs to Bandit

## Production Notes

### Tone Balance

The screenplay walks a precise line between:
- **Dark Comedy:** Absurdist premise, military precision applied to garbage diving
- **Character Drama:** Genuine emotional stakes, trauma backstory
- **Genre Thrills:** Attack sequence, crash choreography

### Key Technical Challenges

1. **Raccoon Performance:** Requires either trained animal work, puppet/animatronic, or VFX
2. **Truck Interior:** Extended dialogue scene requires coverage options
3. **Crash Sequence:** Stunt coordination, fence destruction, party extras
4. **Match Cut:** Eyes close/open requires precise framing continuity

### Tonal Guidance for Director

- Danny's confession scene must play as genuine, not ironic
- The raccoon attack should be terrifying, not slapstick
- "It's fine" refrain should escalate in desperation, then disappear
- Final "I'm okay" should feel earned, not cheesy
